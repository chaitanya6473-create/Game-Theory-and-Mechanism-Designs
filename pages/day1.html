Training Summary

The training began with an overview of game theory and its role in studying situations where the outcome for each participant depends on the choices made by others. We discussed strategic interaction and how game-theoretic models are used in economics, computer science, business, politics, and everyday decision-making. The session introduced the difference between individual decision-making and decisions made in a competitive or cooperative setting.

Learning Objectives / Outcomes

Identify situations involving strategic interdependence and explain why the choices of other participants matter.

Learning Reflection

I learned that game theory provides a structured way to analyze decisions rather than relying only on intuition. The examples helped connect the subject to practical situations.

Key Takeaways

  • Connect the day's concepts with strategic decision-making.
  • Use clear assumptions and structured reasoning when analyzing examples.
  • Review the topic and practice a related problem independently.
